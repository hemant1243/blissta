# BLISSTA STATIC DESIGN SYSTEM v3 (Oct 15 2026)

**This replaces Stop the Thumb v1, Design System v2.1 and v2.4, and the design rules inside Bibles 1, 2 and 2.0 and the Scale Bible wherever they conflict.** The bibles stay as the LIBRARY, the place we port proven layouts from. They are no longer the rulebook.
Why each rule exists: `reports/Winning static ad design system.md` (the research, our results, all 39 complaints traced to their cause).

**The one-line diagnosis:** the AI starts from a template or a mood instead of the message, never has to justify an element, and never looks at its own render before spending credits. v3 fixes the ORDER of decisions.

---

## PART A: THE 12 QUESTIONS EVERY AD MUST ANSWER BEFORE ANY PROMPT IS WRITTEN

Write the answers out for each ad, every time, even for variants. No answers, no render.

1. **Who is he, what moment is he in, and what does he feel?** One line each, in his words.
2. **Which ONE lever is this?** Belief-destroyer / scroll-stopper / choice-obviator / proof-visualizer. One only.
3. **How aware is he?** Unaware / problem / solution (has tried X) / product / most aware. This sets the skeleton, the product size and the offer (Part C).
4. **What is the ONE thing that stops the thumb?** His number, his moment, a familiar object, his words, a huge number. Name it.
5. **Which proven ad am I porting, and what exactly am I borrowing?** Name it from the 70 breakdowns or our winners. Borrow layout and light only. Never invent a layout.
6. **Which skeleton (S1–S6)?** Has another concept in this batch already claimed it? If yes, pick another.
7. **What is the picture?** A real moment from his life, or a proof object he can check. Metaphors, mood shots, decorative anatomy and random AI couples are banned. Cover test: hide all the words. Does the picture alone still show the moment or the proof?
8. **Eye path 1 → 5:** what he sees first, second, third...
9. **Element ledger:** every element, the buyer question it answers, and what's lost if it's removed. If nothing is lost, delete it.
10. **What colors, and WHY?** Run the function chain (Part D). If the answer is "to match the product" or "it feels trustworthy", it's wrong.
11. **Exact sizes?** From the type scale (Part D), checked in the free wireframe first. Never guessed.
12. **Swap test:** put this ad's words on the previous concept's design. Does anything look wrong? If not, they're the same concept. Redesign.

## PART B: SETTLED RULES (each one ends a contradiction between our old docs)

| # | Topic | THE rule now |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Background | One color is allowed only as a real material with texture and light falloff (linen, plaster, paper, stone, oak). Flat untextured fill is banned. Plain grounds are for proof, offer and type-led ads; belief and scroll ads get a real scene. |
| 2 | Palette across a batch | **Lock the system, vary the ground.** Locked across the whole brand: font, label accent, price pill style. The background or scene changes on every concept and every variant. Only when scaling a PROVEN winner, keep its ground and vary the hook (90% same). |
| 3 | Accent | ONE accent, sampled from the product's own label, on at most 3 named items. Red only as a functional mark on a real object (a strikethrough, a marker circle), and it counts toward the 3. Never red and green together. Never a sticker on the product. |
| 4 | Picking color | Function chain only (Part D). No feelings→color tables. |
| 5 | Type | One heavy, normal-width geometric sans. Locked: **Montserrat 800 headlines / 600 facts** until Hemant picks a licensed alternative. Two weights, sentence case. CAPS only for a ≤5-word punch, or when the real object prints in caps (receipt, tape). Serif or handwriting only when it's the real object's own lettering (a letter, apology notice, Sharpie). No condensed fonts. |
| 6 | Offer | One offer object plus at most one risk-reversal line, set as its own object beside the product. No button, no bottom bar. Vary the offer type across variants (price anchor / daily cost / risk reversal). Cold: risk reversal or nothing. In offer tests, the offer IS the ad. |
| 7 | "Native" | Native = a real physical object or photo from his life (receipt, scorecard, BP cuff, pill organizer, golf card). Phone UI (Notes, iMessage, tweet) is OFF for Corbel. Max 1 in 15 for MB/PainBloc until one wins. |
| 8 | Hook length | The first line stops the scroll by itself, ≤6 words, set largest. Scroll/belief ads ≤10 words total. Proof fact stacks may run 11–18 words when the extra words qualify (e.g. "for fibro fatigue") or are a checkable number. |
| 9 | Prompt length | Word count doesn't matter; completeness does. Fill every block of the template (Part E) with measured values. Delete mood words. |
| 10 | Facts | Numbers come only from the live site or label. Nothing old: no 120/90/60 days, no geography, no 600mg. |
| 11 | Picture vs fact stack | The picture is either a real moment (belief/scroll) or a proof object (proof/choice). In a fact stack, the product IS the proof object. |
| 12 | Splits | Two things go in ONE continuous scene (same surface, same light, soft dissolve or light-temperature change). Never two photos glued with a seam. Never panels. |
| 13 | Product size | Proof 40–48% of canvas height · choice bundle 50–60% of width · belief scene 28–36% · scroll artifact 20–30%. Never under 25% when the ad argues from the product. Always inside the logic (end of the hairlines, the "after" side, in the scene), never a corner sticker. |
| 14 | Aspect ratio | Design each ratio natively. In 4:5 the extra 270 px goes to the picture, never to an extra text band. |
| 15 | Reviews | Only verbatim reviews from blissta.co, with the name as given. No fake review cards. Stars only if the real score is shown. |
| 16 | Maroon/oxblood, navy | Not brand grounds. A color appears only as the real color of something in the scene, or as the label accent. |
| 17 | Copy | Approved copy goes in word for word. Design never adds, cuts or improves it. If it doesn't fit, change the skeleton or size; otherwise stop and ask. No eyebrow tags ("For men 50+ who...") unless they're in the copy. No em dashes. |
| 18 | Variants | Exploration batch: every concept has a different skeleton and a different scene. Hook triplets on one image are ONLY for refining a concept that already won. |

## PART C: SIX SKELETONS (all ported from tested or approved ads) + WHEN TO USE THEM

Zones are positions inside ONE image, never horizontal bands. Pixel values are for 1080 wide; on 4:5 the extra height goes to the picture.

| Skeleton | Lever · proof it works | Layout (1080×1080 / 1080×1350) | Type route |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S1 Fact stack** | Proof · Ultra Blue winner ~$26 CPA (one variation ~$9), Oct 15 approved | Headline top center (y 60–330 / 72–420). Product big, centered, 40–46% of height, base near the bottom. 4–6 short facts, 2–3 per side, each tied to the product with a 3 px hairline. No numbers or bullets. Price pill bottom-right. | Code-set type on a textured plate |
| **S2 Real-moment scene** | Belief/scroll · PainBloc caregiver ads (top spenders) | Full-bleed photo of his place, one light source. Headline left-aligned on a quiet scene surface (wall, sky, doorway), width ≤720. Product on a real surface, 300–390 px tall, lower-right third, in the warm "answer" light. | In-model if the type sits on a calm surface; code if the surface is busy |
| **S3 Proof object** | Belief + proof · Corbel receipt ~$8 CPA, scorecard sales, golf card | The printed object (receipt, scorecard, cuff, golf card) fills 55–70% of the frame on a real surface. Headline ≤10 words, or the object's own title. Product beside it, same surface and light. One offer object. | Numbers on the object: code or composite. Headline can be in-model |
| **S4 Scroll artifact** | Scroll · Sharpie on skin, warning tape, apology notice | The artifact fills 65–80%. ≤7 words written in the artifact's own hand or print. Product 20–30%, in hand or at the edge. No designed headline. | In-model (the lettering belongs to the object). 2 rerolls max, then composite |
| **S5 Offer lead** | Choice · offer tests, most aware | The math is the headline ("65¢ a day", "5 for $97.99"), 130–160 px. 5 units standing naturally on one surface, 50–60% of width. One price device plus one risk reversal. No stickers, no button. | Code-set prices |
| **S6 One-scene comparison** | Choice/proof · scorecard pattern | Theirs on the left in flat cool light, ours on the right in warm key light, on the same continuous surface. Max 4 rows at 44–52 px. Any divider is a drawn line. | Code-set rows. Real competitor labels need Hemant's OK |

| He is... | Lead lever | Skeleton | Product | Offer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Scroll / identity | S4, S2 | 20–30%, in the scene | None |
| Problem-aware | Belief (blame the bottle, never him) | S2, S3 | 28–36% | None, or the risk-reversal line |
| Solution-aware (has tried X) | Belief + proof | S3, S6, S1 | 35–45% | Daily cost OR risk reversal |
| Product-aware | Proof | S1, S3 | 40–48% | Price pill + risk reversal |
| Most aware / retargeting | Choice | S5 | Bundle 50–60% width | The offer is the ad |

**Message → device:**
- His number (152/94, A1C, pain score) → the object that shows it.
- A failed solution → a receipt or scorecard.
- Dose/spec superiority → fact stack.
- Caregiver or relationship → real moment.
- An embarrassing moment → an object from that moment.
- Mechanism → only when it doubles as proof in one image (PainBloc mechanism statics sit at $67–74 CPA).

**15-ad batch density:** 3 minimal / 5 light / 5 medium / 1 medium-heavy / 1 heavy.

## PART D: TYPE SCALE, COLOR CHAIN, PRODUCT COLORS

**Type scale (canvas px; the feed shows ~0.35–0.40× this; the 55+ readable floor is 44 px):**

| Role | 1080×1080 | 1080×1350 | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Headline ≤6 words | 130–160 | 140–170 | 800 weight, line-height 1.05–1.10, 2–3 lines |
| Headline 7–10 words | 100–125 | 110–135 | Break lines by meaning |
| Headline 11–18 words | Stop phrase 104–120 + rest 64–80 | Stop phrase 110–130 + rest 72–88 | Stop phrase ≤6 words on lines 1–2 |
| Facts | 44–52 | 46–56 | ≤5 words, ≤2 lines each; gap ≥ 1× type size |
| Price pill | 48–60 | 52–64 | Pill height ≈1.7× type; one corner radius per ad |
| Risk reversal | 40–48 | 44–52 | One line, never inside the pill |
| Hairlines | 3 px | 3 px | Stop 12–20 px short of the product |
| Margins | 60 all round | 64 sides / 72 top / 96 bottom | Nothing must-read in the bottom 96 px of 4:5 |

**Fit check before choosing a size:** a 952 px line holds about 10 characters at 160 px, 13 at 120, 16 at 100, 21 at 76, 33 at 48. Hierarchy is measured on the stop phrase: ≥2.5× the fact size, target 3–4×.

**Color function chain** (in this order, never an emotion table):
1. Sample the product label (hue + lightness).
2. Pick a ground that is **opposite in lightness**, outside the product's hue family, and made of a real material from his world.
3. Text at max contrast: #111111 on light grounds, warm white on dark scenes. ≥4.5:1 everywhere, ≥7:1 under 64 px.
4. The label accent on ≤3 named items.
5. Light temperature: cold for the problem, warm for the answer.

Max 4 colors (ground, text, accent, product). No yellow on white, blue vs green, or pastel pairs carrying meaning (55+ eyes confuse them). Separate states by lightness.

| Product | Good grounds | Banned grounds | Accent |
|---|---|---|---|
| MB Ultra Blue (navy/blue pouch) | Warm off-white linen, cream plaster, light oak, warm stone, morning kitchen counter | Cobalt, navy, any blue; flat beige "luxury" | Label navy: pill + 1 phrase (+1 max) |
| Corbel (navy glass, black cap, gold) | Light warm surfaces. Dark slate only with a rim light on the glass | Navy (invisible); the same cream template every time; oxblood | Label gold OR navy, one per ad |
| PainBloc | Sample the label, go opposite in lightness | The bottle's own hue family; flat saturated navy | Sampled label color |

## PART E: THE PROMPT (describes a photograph, not a layout)

```text
REFERENCES  Image 1 = [product] packshot: copy its shape, colors, cap and every label word exactly; do not redraw label text.
            Image 2 (optional) = [library winner]: borrow composition and light direction only.
CANVAS      [1:1 | 4:5], one continuous full-bleed photograph, edge to edge.
SCENE       [his real place + the one surface the product stands on + at most one prop, named].
LIGHT/LENS  One [window/lamp] light from [direction], [cool 5600K problem | warm 3200K answer], [lens, depth of field], real contact shadow.
PRODUCT     [Image 1 product] standing on [surface], [x]% of frame height, [position relative to scene objects], label facing camera.
TYPE        Route A: Render only the text in quotes, spelled exactly, in a heavy geometric sans-serif, sentence case, color #[hex]:
              line 1 "[...]" line 2 "[...]" (cap height ≈ [n]% of frame height), left-aligned on [named surface].
            Route B: The [named surface] stays smooth and evenly lit; the photograph contains no lettering anywhere.
COLOR       Ground material [linen/plaster/oak] #[hex]; text #[hex]; accent #[label hex] appears only on [item(s)].
KEEP        Only the quoted text appears; Image 1 label unchanged; one light direction; one continuous surface.
```

**Banned words in prompts** (the model draws them literally, or they invite rejected looks): band, strip, bar, panel, section, zone, header, footer, "top N% plain", poster, badge, chip, sticker, eyebrow, luxury, premium feel, and any emotion word standing in for a color ("trustworthy blue").

Every prompt stands alone. Never "same as V1".

**Type route:**
- **Code sets:** fact lists of 3+ items, any number/price/mg/% under ~100 px, hook tests on one approved plate, anything that failed one reroll, everything on plain textured grounds (S1, S5).
- **The image model sets:** a ≤12-word headline on a scene surface with every line locked in quotes, and lettering that belongs to an object (Sharpie, receipt print, tape).
- **Composite the real photo:** any garbled label.
- **For Mark's 2K route:** Claude delivers the type layer as a transparent PNG at 2160 px wide (all px ×2).

## PART F: THE NINE PASSES (in order, every ad)

1. **Intake:** lock the copy, product, facts, awareness level, lever and message type.
2. **Strategy card:** Part A questions 1–4 + the eye path.
3. **Port:** name the reference and what's borrowed. To beat sameness: "list 5 library formats that could carry this, with the % chance you'd default to each; drop any skeleton already claimed in this batch".
4. **Element ledger:** Part A question 9.
5. **Spec:** zones, sizes, hexes, type route.
6. **Free wireframe** (zero credits): gray boxes + the real copy at spec sizes in the locked font, viewed at 100% and at 35%. In the ops chat: `node design/typeset.js <spec> <out>`, which also runs the number checks (fit, floors, margins, overlap, contrast, hierarchy). Fix fit and hierarchy HERE. *"Why are you wasting my credit?" ends here.*
7. **Prompt:** Part E.
8. **Render ONE.**
9. **Critique** (Part G) → fix only what failed (max 2 rerolls) → Hemant's OK → then variants. Mark only receives prompts that passed checks 1–13 on Claude's own render.

## PART G: CRITIQUE, ONE PRINCIPLE PER PASS, ON THE RENDERED IMAGE

Look at the PNG before re-reading the prompt. Compare against the approved anchors (Higgsfield jobs c2b4af39, d3e5d723, c2d7ff20, 0176e83f, 6138b3cf) and the rejected Oct 15 renders. Each check returns pass or fail, where on the image, and one fix.

1. **Copy fidelity:** OCR vs the locked copy; numbers vs the live facts.
2. **2-second message:** at 35% size, can you state who, the moment and the feeling?
3. **One focal point:** at 35% + blur, exactly one dominant thing.
4. **Hierarchy:** stop phrase ≥2.5× the facts; the product is the largest object.
5. **Floors and fit:** nothing must-read under 44 px; nothing inside the margins.
6. **Spacing:** gap between facts ≥1× type size; one left edge per column; hairlines, not numbers.
7. **Type system:** one family, ≤2 weights, sentence case, no condensed.
8. **Color budget:** ≤4 colors; accent on ≤3 items; ground outside the product's hue; order holds in grayscale.
9. **Contrast:** ≥4.5:1 everywhere, ≥7:1 under 64 px.
10. **Continuous scene:** no bands, strips, panels, seams or glued photos; one light direction.
11. **Product:** label intact; size per lever; inside the logic; nothing stuck on it.
12. **Ledger:** every visible element has a buyer reason; extras removed.
13. **Offer:** one offer object (+ ≤1 risk reversal); none on cold; no button.
14. **Native register:** squint. Does it read as a post, or as a banner/template?
15. **Compliance:** no em dashes, country, cure/treat/replace claims, fake reviews, "talk to your doctor".
16. **Batch (batch level):** thumbnail grid. The swap test passes between concepts; hook triplets look related, not identical.

## OPEN DECISIONS FOR HEMANT (the AI must not decide these)

- **Corbel 20,000 FU:** the Drive spec sheet says the physical label reads 10,000 FU. Check a real bottle before printing 20,000 on any ad.
- **MB 14mg per serving:** older notes say gummies shipped at 5mg. Confirm against the current label.
- **Condition names in headlines and real competitor bottles:** live ads use both; the written compliance doc bans both. Pick one.
- **Star ratings:** allowed with the real score, or never?
- **The font:** Montserrat is the free default. Swap in a licensed brand font if you want one; lock it either way.
