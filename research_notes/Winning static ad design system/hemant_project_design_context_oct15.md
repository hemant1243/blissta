## 0 · Who makes the final image
- **AI makes it, not Canva or Figma.**
- **Claude** writes standalone prompts and renders in Higgsfield, Nano Banana 2, 1:1, 1K (about 1.5 credits each).
  - The product is saved as a reference element (Ultra Blue pouch, PainBloc bottle, Corbel, Gluco Gone).
  - Claude renders one first and waits for an OK.
- **Mark** (static designer) gets the prompts as a Slack canvas.
  - He renders in Gemini (Google AI Ultra, Nano Banana Pro / Nano Banana 2, product photo attached, 2K) and makes 150–200+ statics a day.
  - When the small label print garbles, he pastes the real bottle photo over it.
- **"AI makes the picture, code sets the type" is already allowed but barely used.** Design System v2.4 rule 14 says Claude can set or fix type in Python. v2.4 section 5 and Stop the Thumb decision 11 say: if Nano Banana garbles text, render the scene clean and set type by hand.
- When every headline line is locked in the prompt, Nano Banana 2 sets it correctly. What breaks is small print (labels, numbers like "2,7700mg").
- **Almost all the complaints are design DECISIONS, not text rendering.**

## 2 · The 4 levers and 7 durability traits
**Each winner has one main lever. Stacking two dilutes both.**
1. **Belief-destroyer** (43 of 113, now dominant): "It wasn't low T. It was high estrogen."
2. **Scroll-stopper** (37 of 113): Sharpie on skin, Notes screenshot, warning tape, press-release apology.
3. **Choice-obviator** (28 of 113): strikethrough, BOGO, us vs them, dose ladder, risk reversal. A layer, rarely the lead.
4. **Proof-visualizer** (19 of 113, the longest runners: 365/303/281 days): timeline, x-ray glow, before/after, stat equivalency.

**The 7 durability traits (every survivor has at least 3):**
- a universal lever, not novelty
- many variations rotating inside one concept
- an identity hook, not a feature hook
- photo-native or a proprietary look, not a designed graphic
- verifiable proof
- sub-avatar specificity
- native register for the platform

## 4 · Bible 2.0: 5 meta-patterns
1. **Belief-destroyer dominates.** "It's not X, it's Y."
2. **The avatar's own words beat brand voice.**
3. **Native beats designed beats polished.** Sharpie on a foot beats $5K photography; an email screenshot ran 303 days.
4. **Offer math is a layer, not a format.**
5. **Iteration discipline is the moat:** 5–65 variations of one concept.

**Belief patterns:** B1 It's not X it's Y · B2 almost banned · B3 suppressed authority · B4 "I wish my doctor told me" · B5 "I ditched X years of [drug]" · B6 geographic reframe · B7 news hijack · B8 press-release apology · B9 founder letter · B10 identity reaction · B11 curiosity story · B12 catastrophe avoidance (288 days) · B13 reverse reframe · B14 faith hook.

**Scroll patterns:** S1 Sharpie on body · S2 Notes · S3 email screenshot · S4 fake sensitive-content overlay · S5 warning tape · S6 whiteboard · S7 4-word tagline · S8 camera-roll UGC · S9 surreal hybrid object · S10 cartoon transformation.

**Choice patterns:** C1 us vs them · C2 BOGO · C3 price anchor · C4 dose ladder · C5 risk reversal · C6 pricing manifesto · C7 routine calendar.

**Proof patterns:** P1 timeline photos · P2 diagram with red glow · P3 split pipe · P4 before/after · P5 cartoon before/after · P6 hand-drawn sketch · P7 product + 4–6 stat callouts with thin lines (= the Ultra Blue winner format) · P8 mechanism chain.

## 5 · Bible Part 1: 12 laws and 25 archetypes
**Laws:**
1. Under 10 words of main message.
2. One message, one visual, one action.
3. Specificity.
4. Match density to format.
5. Native beats polished.
6. The image amplifies the headline, never competes.
7. Benefit-led; keep the problem brief (cold = Zone 1–2).
8. One focal point.
9. The avatar's exact words.
10. Reframe before you sell.
11. A named source beats general authority.
12. The scroll test.

**Archetypes:** 01 Timeline · 02 Warehouse excuse · 03 Strikethrough + emotional outcome · 04 Floating callouts (3–6 chips with connector lines, prefer 4) · 05 Them vs us (max 4 rows) · 06 Escalation ladder · 07 Stat equivalency · 08 Official apology · 09 Notes screenshot · 10 Handwritten (3–7 words) · 11 Tweet card · 12 Warning tape · 13 Mechanism diagram (2–3 labels, dark) · 14 VIRAL banner · 15 Minimal statement (60–80% white space, 2–5 words) · 16 Big reframe · 17 Final-hours stack · 18 Religious hook · 19 Ogilvy manifesto · 20 It's back · 21 Multi-bottle stack · 22 Chip grid · 23 BOGO · 24 Cartoon transformation · 25 Question hook.

**Stacking rule:** one pattern per layer (hook layer, proof layer, offer layer). Two patterns in the same role cannibalize each other.

## 6 · Bible v2
- **21 formats:** time contrast, avatar call-out, testimonial quote, Sharpie outcome, suppressed-science document, Notes diary, mom's kitchen table, 6-icon hero, cost compare split, aspirational spotlight, mechanism two-panel, "this is NOT" reframe, stack refugee receipts, warning tape, heritage document, real photo + quote, native iPhone proof (blue pee), symptom checklist, enemy named, before/after, generational witness.
- **Density per 15 ads:** 3 minimal / 5 light / 5 medium / 1 medium-heavy / 1 heavy.
- **Background DNA:** "the background is the emotional engine". Palettes by angle; always specify texture, light and vignette.
- **12 killers:** flat single-color background · same palette on 15+ ads · hook over 10 words · two claims · vague visual spec · no specificity · dark hook on cold traffic · no offer · repeated template · direct medical claim · real brand visible · wrong offer tier.

## 7 · Scale Bible (28 competitor rips)
- **8-figure brands lock 2–3 background systems for the whole catalog.** Healthletic: neon navy / cream aged paper / periwinkle clinical. Maïa: oil-painted cream / cream grid. Resilia: pure black / cream editorial.
- **1 concept × 3 hooks:** same visual, three hooks attacking three different beliefs.
- **Micro-variation:** keep 90% of the DNA, shift 10% (light angle, bottle scale 24/26/28%, one prop, rim light, a 2–3° tilt). The series test: with the headlines covered, the three look related but not identical.
- **Props:** bottle + 1 prop max.

## 8 · Stop the Thumb v1 (Oct 6): 12 decisions
1. The message in 2 seconds (who, moment, feeling).
2. One stopper (huge number, his moment, a familiar object, a color jolt, his words, their buyers' words).
3. Visual or none: only proof, transformation, the real object, or a clean metaphor. Use the removal test; otherwise bottle + type.
4. Format by message type.
5. Color: one dominant background, one text color, one accent on one thing, a one-color CTA, max 4 colors.
6. Type: one family, two weights, headline ≥2.5x the subline, one highlight, break lines by meaning.
7. Write the eye path 1–5 before placing anything.
8. Offer + proof.
9. The set: same device, different layout per version.
10. The small things: kerning, one left edge, one corner radius, one shadow, the thumbnail test at 20%, the grayscale test, the feed test.
11. The prompt.
12. The kill test.

## 9 · Design System v2.4 (Oct 8–10, built from rejections; overrides v1)
- **The picture sells first.** One dominant image. Cover test: cover every word; the picture alone must show the moment and the person.
- **One feeling on purpose.** Polite = invisible.
- **Three blocks:** hook, picture, product + offer.
- **One offer per ad**, varied across variants (price anchor / daily cost / risk reversal). No button on the image.
- **One smooth full-bleed scene.** No stacked bands, split backgrounds or panels. Timelines go inside one image with soft dissolves.
- **The background changes with every message.** A real place from his life; cold light for the problem, warm for the result. The background never matches the bottle color.
- **Tie the type treatment to the picture.**
- **No two concepts share a skeleton.** If you can swap the words between two concepts and nothing looks wrong, they're one concept.
- **Port a proven layout and understand why it works.** Never invent layouts from descriptions; never rebuild a winner without its image.
- **A real moment beats a metaphor.** The smoke alarm metaphor was predicted #1 and got no spend.
- **Kill list:** colors from a feelings table · navy everywhere · one flat sand background on every ad · text tables as the visual · fake review cards · the same sticker + pill + proof line on every ad · all-caps headlines · decorative anatomy diagrams.

## 11 · Every design complaint, counted (Sep 30 – Oct 15)
- **Same template or skeleton across versions/concepts: 8.** Same layout as X · 3 versions with the same split, palette, font and footer · all headline + bottle + sticker · PEA batch 1 skeleton rebuilt twice · MB 6 concepts on one skeleton · 5 formats of one idea called 5 concepts · C2 V1 copy-pasted · Corbel 5 on one cream template.
- **Stacked bands or forced background zones: 5.** Paper band + photo + wood strip · band + 3 panels · "top 25% plain wall" renders as a band · Corbel two photos glued.
- **Forced or wrong color: 6.** Muted beige "luxury" invisible · colors from a feelings table, navy everywhere · cobalt forced twice · two-color CTA · red sticker fighting the brand.
- **Forced visuals, props or metaphors: 5.** Notepads, Notes screens, exam rooms · smoke alarm · hands holding papers in a truck · Ogilvy pastiche · invented layouts.
- **Type and hierarchy: 4.** List jammed together · font too big · all-caps headlines · meaningless numbering.
- **Design with no buyer reason: 3.** "design is never for design's sake" · "every element needs a reason".
- **Ignored references, invented instead of porting: 3.** Rebuilt a winner without its image · asked for an image when the breakdowns were enough · "we steal proven formats, we don't invent".
- **Touched approved copy while designing: 3.** Added "Now:" · cut copy to fix a crowded render · redesign changed the messaging.
- **Eyebrow or callout tags: 2.** "For men 50+ who..." on every ad · callout as its own block.

**The pattern behind all of it:** the AI starts from a template or a mood, not from the message, and nothing forces it to justify each element before it renders.

## 13 · Hard rules that don't change
- Approved copy goes in word for word. Design never adds, cuts or improves it.
- No em dashes on the image. No country, state or "Made in USA". No "talk to your doctor". No cure, treat, reverse or replace-a-medication claims.
- **Numbers come from the live site or label only:**
  - Ultra Blue: 14mg per serving (7mg/gummy × 2), 20mg vitamin C, 99.9% pure, cold processed.
  - PainBloc: 1,200mg micronized PEA.
  - Corbel: 20,000 FU.
  - 5 for $97.99 (subscription), 65¢ a day, 30-day money-back, 147,000+ customers.
- Every prompt stands alone; never "same as V1". Render one first and wait for an OK.
- The product is big and inside the logic (at the end of the arrows, on the "after" side, in the scene), never a sticker in a corner. The offer is its own object.
