# Attention and Scroll-Stopping: Visual Mechanics of Static Image Ads in the Meta Mobile Feed

Research note scope: static image ads in Facebook/Instagram mobile feed. Video findings are included only where they plausibly transfer. Measured evidence and practitioner opinion are labeled. Research date: Oct 2026. Overall caveat: Meta's own published attention research is old (2015–2016) and mostly about video. Most 2023–2026 "data" on statics comes from vendors and agencies, with no published methodology.

---

## 1. What eye-tracking and attention studies say about how people scan feed ads

### Takeaway
Feed ads get very little attention. Measured attention is about 1–2 seconds per ad, and about 70% to 80% of "viewable" impressions get none. People recognise and recall content within 0.25 s. They also learn to skip anything that signals "ad": eye-tracking shows sponsored Instagram posts get about 25% fewer fixations than organic posts. Users disengage soon after their eyes land on an ad cue such as a CTA button or "Sponsored" label. The decision is made in a fraction of a second, mostly on the main image area and on whether the post reads as an ad.

### Cited Findings
**Time budget (measured; mostly older)**
- Independent Fors Marsh tests, cited by Facebook, found people can recall mobile News Feed content after only 0.25 seconds of exposure. This is Facebook's characterisation, about 2015–2016. The original Fors Marsh report is not public. — [Facebook for Business: Capture attention with updated features for video ads](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/updated-features-for-video-ads); [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/facebook-why-mobile-video-ads-must-work-fast/446217/)
- Facebook data (about 2016): people spend 1.7 s with a piece of content on mobile vs 2.5 s on desktop. — [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/facebook-why-mobile-video-ads-must-work-fast/446217/)
- Facebook/Nielsen (video, about 2015–16): up to 47% of a campaign's value came in the first 3 s, and up to 74% in the first 10 s. A separate Nielsen study tied 38% of brand recall and 25% of purchase intent to video impressions under 2 seconds. This transfers to statics as "the message must land at a glance." — [Facebook for Business](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/updated-features-for-video-ads); [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/facebook-why-mobile-video-ads-must-work-fast/446217/)
- Lumen Research eye-tracking (older blog posts, about 2016–2019): Facebook ads averaged about 2.1 s of attention vs about 1.3 s for typical display ads. People were "far more likely to remember a brand if they look at an ad for more than 2 seconds." On Facebook the ad's viewable window was about 8 s, vs about 20 s on other sites. — [Lumen Research blog](https://lumen-research.com/blog/the-future-of-facebook-is-lumen-according-to-mark-ritson/)
- Lumen, single month (November): 54% of viewable Facebook ads were actually looked at, vs 42% on YouTube. An earlier Lumen figure says about 30% of viewable in-feed ads get looked at, vs about 20% for display. — [Lumen blog: Facebook beats YouTube](https://lumen-research.com/blog/facebook-beats-youtube-for-most-noticed-advertising-this-november-with-bt-com-third/); [Lumen](https://lumen-research.com/?p=821)
- Karen Nelson-Field (Amplified Intelligence), based on camera-based attention measurement:
  - On average only about 20% of impressions are actively seen.
  - About 75% of "viewable" inventory gets zero attention.
  - Attention "drops sharply after the first second."
  - Time-in-view has little relationship to attention.
  — [Bizcommunity](https://www.bizcommunity.com/article/karen-nielsen-field-the-attention-deficit-how-to-overcome-it-510470a); [The Drum 2025](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2025/05/07/the-next-attention-revolution-here-says-dr-karen-nelson-field)

**Native-ad blindness on Instagram (measured, 2025, peer-reviewed)**
- Hübner, Thalmann and Henseler (Frontiers in Psychology, 2025) eye-tracked 152 participants scrolling a mock Instagram feed of 29 posts, 8 of them sponsored:
  - Sponsored posts drew fewer fixations: 5.00 vs 6.52 for organic, about 25% fewer (η² = 0.40).
  - They also drew slightly less dwell time: about 6.57 s vs 6.74 s in Table 4. The paper's text and table disagree on the means.
  - The content image drew the most fixations of any element: 3.70 on sponsored posts vs 4.69 on organic.
  — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12380683/)
- Same study, on ad cues:
  - Early fixation on the CTA button or the disclosure was tied to lower dwell on the main content in 25 of 31 tests (80.6%). The authors say these "flags" "activate learned avoidance."
  - The cues participants said made them recognise an ad were: explicit product in the image (178 mentions), disclosure (95), brand logo as profile picture (81), CTA button (77), clear immediately recognisable content (59) and verification badge (27).
  — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12380683/)
- Sample caveat: the participants were young (mean age 25), so the results may not transfer to a 55+ audience. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12380683/)
- A Radboud University thesis (2023, 62 participants, not peer-reviewed) found that logos placed in the "area of action" of a post attract more visual attention than logos in the designated sponsor area. Significance was not conclusive. — [Radboud thesis](https://theses.ubn.ru.nl/handle/123456789/16503)

**Banner blindness (NN/g)**
- NN/g "Banner Blindness Revisited" (2018, 26 participants per page) confirmed the effect first documented in 1997 and again in 2007:
  - Users barely looked at anything in an ad-like position or ad-like styling.
  - Right-rail attention was 33 times lower than its size warranted.
  - Every participant ignored an inline promo whose shape, coloured background and embedded text signalled "ad."
  — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/banner-blindness-old-and-new-findings/)
- On mobile, inline ads are harder to avoid because they are large relative to the screen. But mobile users also mistook large images or graphics for ads and scrolled past them. — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/banner-blindness-old-and-new-findings/)
- Older NN/g commentary (2007): text-based ads with contrasting colours, such as white text on red, were less likely to be ignored. "They're looking at them if they're text." — [ClickZ summary](https://clickz.com/research-internet-users-plagued-by-banner-blindness/58428/)

**Faces and gaze (measured, vendor)**
- VidMob analysed 1.1M video assets across Facebook, Instagram, Snapchat and Google, 2018–2020. These are video opening frames, so the result transfers only partly to statics:
  - Direct-to-camera gaze was linked to a CTR increase of more than 2.7% on Facebook/Instagram in 2019–2020. The effect reversed in 2018.
  - Direct gaze was linked to a 6.67% decline in view rate across platforms.
  - Direct gaze plus a positive expression helped app installs.
  - Indirect gaze helped sports, lifestyle and medical content.
  - The article's impression counts are internally inconsistent.
  — [MediaPost, Dec 2021](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/369355/how-eye-gaze-in-ads-affects-creative-performance.html)
- VidMob on Snap (2022, not Meta): high-contrast, low-text ads performed well. A clear CTA in the first 2 s gave a 71% higher swipe-up rate. — [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/fr/news-release/2022/05/05/2436697/0/en/High-Contrast-Low-Text-Ads-Perform-Well-on-Snap-Ads-for-Key-Industries.html)
- VidMob Meta holiday study (2021; 3.2B impressions, video): text in the first frame gave a 3x sales lift vs text that appeared later. This supports front-loading the message. — [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2021/11/10/2331651/0/en/Creative-Elements-Found-to-Have-a-Direct-Impact-on-e-Commerce-Holiday-Campaign-Performance.html)

### Inferences
- A static gets roughly 0.25–2 s. In that time the eye lands on the dominant image area first, because the content image gets the most fixations. It then checks for ad cues. If the image screams "ad" (studio product shot, logo, CTA styling), avoidance starts before any copy is read. The design goal is a dominant visual that reads as content, with one message legible in that window.
- "Explicit product in image" was the most-cited ad-recognition cue. That is a real tension: showing the product helps recall, but the more prominent it is, the more readily the post is seen as an ad.

### Gaps
- No public eye-tracking data compares 4:5 vs 1:1 vs 9:16 statics on scan path, first fixation or dwell. Meta has published none, and no vendor study turned up.
- I found no 2022–2026 Meta/Facebook IQ eye-tracking study specific to static feed ads. Current Lumen and Amplified Intelligence Facebook-specific numbers are gated or unpublished.
- Tobii, EyeSee and MediaScience: no public static-feed-ad findings surfaced in this search.
- I found no rigorous public data on colour salience (for example red vs other colours) for Meta statics.

## 2. What Meta itself recommends for static creative

### Takeaway
Meta's guidance is thin, but it is consistent on five points:
- Design for mobile and sound-off.
- Make the message and brand clear quickly.
- Use 4:5 vertical for single-image Feed ads.
- Keep key elements out of edges that the UI covers.
- Keep image text light.

The 20% text rule was dropped in September 2020: text no longer affects delivery, but Meta still says images with under 20% text generally perform better. Meta has published no hard data on 4:5 vs 1:1, logo placement or text-heavy images for statics.

### Cited Findings
- **Aspect ratio:** Meta's best-practice docs, as quoted by Jon Loomer, say "Vertical 4:5 is recommended for single-image ads to be delivered to the ad placement Facebook Feed." — [Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/qvt/meta-recommends-4x5-aspect-ratio/) (page returned 403 on fetch; quote taken from the search snippet)
- **No public proof that 4:5 beats 1:1:** a third-party analysis says Meta publishes no results showing 4:5 beats 1:1, and the oft-quoted "1% higher CTR" has no traceable source. The case for 4:5 rests on its extra height. — [Admake](https://admakeai.com/blog/4-5-aspect-ratio). Billo claims 4:5 performs "up to 15% better" than square in Feed, but publishes no method. — [Billo](https://billo.app/blog/meta-ads-best-practices/)
- **Carousel cards** are still 1:1, and forcing 4:5 can cause awkward crops in some Facebook surfaces (practitioner view). — [Mediaura](https://mediaura.com/boosting-posts-on-meta-think-twice-before-switching-to-the-45-instagram-grid/)
- **20% text rule:**
  - Removed in September 2020. Facebook "will no longer penalize ads with higher amounts of text in auctions and delivery."
  - It still states that "images with less than 20% text generally perform better."
  - Content policy still applies to the text.
  — [Bronco](https://www.bronco.co.uk/our-ideas/?p=6427); [Adzooma](https://adzooma.com/blog/facebook-advertising-removes-20-text-rule-on-images); [Social Samosa](https://socialsamosa.com/2020/09/facebook-removes-20-percent-text-rule-ads)
- **Meta's mobile-attention guidance** (about 2015–16, written for video; principles are generic):
  - Make brand identity clear early with brand colours, imagery or logos.
  - Design for sound off, conveying the message through on-screen text, logos and product.
  - Front-load product shots, recognisable people or action.
  - Test.
  — [Facebook for Business](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/updated-features-for-video-ads)
- **Sound off:** 80% of people react negatively when feed video plays loudly and unexpectedly, which is why Meta pushes "sound-off" design. Statics are silent by nature. — [Facebook for Business](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/updated-features-for-video-ads)
- **Safe zones:**
  - Feed (Meta help centre, per a secondary source): for Instagram feed ads at 1:1 or 4:5, keep the bottom and side edges free of key elements, text and logos, because the interface can cover them. — [via Segwise/secondary quote of Meta Help Center](https://segwise.ai/blog/75-meta-ad-ideas-creative-testing)
  - 9:16 Stories/Reels (third-party, figures vary): keep critical content out of the top about 14%, the bottom about 20–35% and about 6% on each side. — [Billo](https://billo.app/blog/meta-ads-safe-zones); [Adnova](https://adnova.ai/blogs/meta-ad-safe-zones-guide)
  - Unconfirmed: two vendors report a unified Stories/Reels safe zone and an Ads Manager "Safe Zone Guardrail" overlay in 2026. I could not confirm either in Meta's documentation. — [Billo](https://billo.app/blog/meta-ads-safe-zones)
- **Meta-commissioned SalesBrain neuromarketing study** (about 2015): people are more attentive when consuming media on mobile. — [Facebook IQ "The Thumb is in Charge" (SlideShare)](https://www.slideshare.net/fbmarket/the-thumb-is-in-charge-facebook-iq-magazine-nr-1)

### Inferences
- In practice Meta's guidance for statics boils down to: 4:5, one clear focal point, light text, brand cue present, and nothing important near the edges. Beyond the 2020 statement that "<20% text generally performs better," Meta gives no static-specific performance data on text density or logo size.
- Meta's "brand early" advice conflicts with the native-ad-blindness evidence in Section 1. A resolution consistent with both: put the brand on the product or packaging and in the page name, not as a big logo block.

### Gaps
- Meta's current Creative Best Practices and Blueprint pages could not be fetched (404). I found no 2022–2026 Meta Foresight report specific to static image creative.
- I found no Meta research on logo size or placement, or on product visibility, in statics.

## 3. Pattern interrupts: what visual devices measurably raise thumb-stop

### Takeaway
Hard, public, element-level data for statics is scarce. The best measured evidence is:
- Faces and gaze (VidMob, video): small CTR effects that depend on context.
- Front-loaded text (VidMob, video): 3x sales lift for text in the first frame.
- High contrast with low text (VidMob, Snap).
- Native-looking images: one practitioner dataset shows a 3.4x higher "See More" rate.

Specific devices such as notes-app or iMessage screenshots, red circles, arrows, handwritten Post-its and "found photo" looks are widely used and recommended. However, I found no public dataset isolating their effect. The evidence for them is anecdotal or proprietary (Motion, Foreplay and Atria libraries), and practitioners warn they wear out as they become common.

### Cited Findings
- **Native-looking image** (practitioner data, Smart Marketer, $28M spend across own brands and clients; no stats method published):
  - With identical copy, a native-style image had a 1.97% See More Rate vs 0.58% for the comparison image.
  - Native-looking images beat polished visuals, text-overlay statics and video on See More Rate.
  - Caveat from the same source: higher engagement does not guarantee better CPA or ROAS, so check downstream.
  — [Smart Marketer](https://smartmarketer.com/2025-facebook-insights-part-2/)
- **Faces/gaze:** see the VidMob findings in Section 1. Segwise asserts that real faces and believable reactions beat "sterile product shots" "in many tests," but gives no numbers or source. — [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/369355/how-eye-gaze-in-ads-affects-creative-performance.html); [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/mobile-ad-creative-trends.md)
- **Contrast/low text:** VidMob's Snap analysis found high-contrast, low-text ads performed well. — [GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/fr/news-release/2022/05/05/2436697/0/en/High-Contrast-Low-Text-Ads-Perform-Well-on-Snap-Ads-for-Key-Industries.html)
- **Text in the first frame:** 3x sales lift on Meta video. — [GlobeNewswire (VidMob 2021)](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2021/11/10/2331651/0/en/Creative-Elements-Found-to-Have-a-Direct-Impact-on-e-Commerce-Holiday-Campaign-Performance.html)
- **Native formats named by practitioners:** shaky handheld video, handwritten notes on a messy desk, meme formats and Instagram's built-in font. The handwritten Post-it, once fresh, is now common. — [Modern Retail, Jan 2024](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/dtc-briefing-why-ugly-ads-are-taking-off/)
- **Post-it example:** Marketing Examples cites a "$50K return on a 'Post-it'" ad, crediting Barry Hott. It gives no further method. — [Marketing Examples](https://marketingexamples.com/ads/ugly)
- **Screenshots** are listed as an "ugly ad" type, without numbers. — [Applica](https://applica.agency/blog/why-ugly-ads-win-on-meta-and-why-polished-brand-creative-keeps-losing-in-feed)
- **Static vs video context** (vendor, unverified):
  - A 2025 analysis of 67,000 Facebook ads across 100+ top accounts (Curtis Howland/MisfitMarketing via LinkedIn) found the median account ran about 61% static and 39% video.
  - Benchmarks cited in the same article:

    | Metric | Video | Static |
    |---|---|---|
    | CTR | 1.9% | 1.1% |
    | CPA | $48.20 | $34.50 |

  - Statics have 40–60% lower CPMs but fatigue faster: a refresh cycle of 20–30 days vs 40–60 days for video.
  — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/static-video-ratio-meta-ads.md)
- **Motion's 2025 Creative Trends report** (over $100M spend analysed, plus a survey of DTC advertisers) named "embracing low-fi aesthetics" as one of 7 trends. 68% of advertisers said they plan to increase UGC use. — [Future Commerce podcast summary](https://futurecommerce.com/podcasts/7-creative-trends-shaping-dtc-in-2025); [Upgrowth newsletter](https://upgrowthcommercenewsletter.beehiiv.com/p/re-2025-creative-trends-report)

### Inferences
- The devices practitioners favour (native UI screenshots, handwritten notes, red circles and arrows, found-photo looks) share two mechanisms that fit the Section 1 evidence:
  1. They look like organic content, which avoids ad-recognition cues.
  2. They are high-contrast, single-focus visuals with very few words, which are readable inside the ~1–2 s window.
- Novelty decays as formats spread, as the Post-it example shows. Format rotation matters more than any one device.

### Gaps
- I found no public element-level static datasets from Motion, Foreplay, Atria, Marpipe, AdCreative or Smartly that quantify red circles, arrows, hands-holding-product or notes-app and iMessage screenshots. This data seems to sit inside proprietary tools (Motion's reports are per-account).
- I found no rigorous public evidence on "unexpected object" pattern interrupts for Meta statics.

## 4. The "ugly ads" thesis: claims, evidence, when polished wins, older and health audiences

### Takeaway
The ugly-ads thesis (Barry Hott and others) holds that ads which don't look like ads win, because users screen out ad-looking creative. That claim is consistent with the measured native-ad-blindness and banner-blindness research. However, the performance evidence is almost entirely self-reported:
- course testimonials;
- one brand's estimate that ugly wins 3 of 4 times;
- one agency's cross-account directional figures (statics about 25–30% better, not causal).

Practitioners agree that polished creative can win for premium positioning, high-trust or regulated categories, and product demonstrations. Message and audience matter more than aesthetics. I found no study specific to 55+ audiences or to supplements.

### Cited Findings
**What is claimed (practitioner)**
- Hott's definition: ugly ads are "ads that don't look or feel like traditional ads, making them more relatable and relevant." His line: "These are the ads people don't hate."
- His method: front-load the message instead of building to a reveal; relevance over attention; shoot on a phone; ads can be "uglified."
— [Building Ads with Barry](https://www.buildingadswithbarry.com/)
- Not about deliberately low quality: Hott's mentee says the point is not "filming in a dark room or on a low resolution setting." The core rule is that ads should not look like ads at first glance. — [Alex Cooper](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/7-facebook-advertising-lessons-i-learned-from-barry-hott)
- Brand guidelines: Hott argues rigid brand guidelines make ads look alike. He also says good ads still need a strong hook, story and pitch. — [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/dtc-briefing-why-ugly-ads-are-taking-off/)

**Evidence (self-reported or directional)**
- Pattern Brands (Suze Dowling) tested about 20,000 ad variants in a year. "For three out of every four ads, it is the uglier — or more authentic — ad that wins and is able to scale." — [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/dtc-briefing-why-ugly-ads-are-taking-off/)
- Caraway tests 20–30 new pieces a week; 10–20% of them work. — [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/dtc-briefing-why-ugly-ads-are-taking-off/)
- Testimonials on Hott's course page (self-reported, unverified):

  | Metric | Before | After |
  |---|---|---|
  | Thumb-stop rate | 34% | 48% |
  | CTR | 1.74 | 3.32 |
  | CPC | 1.99 | 1.25 |

  Another testimonial reports roughly 3x click rate and 3–5x conversion rate for a casually shot ad vs polished content.
  — [Building Ads with Barry](https://www.buildingadswithbarry.com/)
- Applica (cross-account directional benchmarks from its managed Meta accounts; explicitly not causal):
  - Phone-shot, feed-native statics showed about 25–30% overall advantage over highly polished brand statics.
  - Video UGC-style showed about 40–50% over polished video.
  — [Applica](https://applica.agency/blog/why-ugly-ads-win-on-meta-and-why-polished-brand-creative-keeps-losing-in-feed)
- Smart Marketer: native-style image See More Rate of 1.97% vs 0.58% for the comparison image. — [Smart Marketer](https://smartmarketer.com/2025-facebook-insights-part-2/)
- Mechanism support (measured): ad cues trigger "learned avoidance" ([Frontiers 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12380683/)), and users skip anything that looks like an ad ([NN/g 2018](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/banner-blindness-old-and-new-findings/)).

**Counter-evidence and nuance**
- Caraway's Jordan Nathan says ugly ads don't always beat professional ones, and some customers prefer premium, studio-shot content. Caraway still runs about 5 polished shoots a month. — [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/dtc-briefing-why-ugly-ads-are-taking-off/)
- Alex Greifeld estimates visual format is the least important factor in ad success: about 70% comes from understanding the audience and the message. — [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/dtc-briefing-why-ugly-ads-are-taking-off/)
- "Authentic" is a moving target: once-fresh formats such as Post-its become common. — [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/dtc-briefing-why-ugly-ads-are-taking-off/)
- Polished still wins for:
  - brand trust in risk-perceived categories, where polish signals the company is "real and regulated";
  - premium positioning;
  - demonstrating a visual result or interface.
  Also judge on downstream CPA, not just CTR: "a creative that wins attention but loses downstream conversion is a false positive." — [Applica](https://applica.agency/blog/why-ugly-ads-win-on-meta-and-why-polished-brand-creative-keeps-losing-in-feed)
- Engagement wins (See More, CTR) may not carry through to CPA or ROAS. — [Smart Marketer](https://smartmarketer.com/2025-facebook-insights-part-2/)
- The Frontiers authors note a trade-off between short-term engagement (blending in) and transparency. Ads that go unnoticed as ads can still earn engagement comparable to friends' posts. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12380683/)
- Commercial bias: Hott sells "make ugly ads" merchandise and a course. — [Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/dtc-briefing-why-ugly-ads-are-taking-off/); [Building Ads with Barry](https://www.buildingadswithbarry.com/)

**Older (55+) audiences**
- Smart Marketer ($28M spend) reports older users show higher CTR, See More rate and thumb-stop rate, but face higher CPMs and fatigue faster at high frequency. An ad featuring an older woman beat one featuring a younger woman on CTR. The source recommends:
  - longer, more informative copy and slower-paced narrative video;
  - 3–5 variations per concept;
  - segmenting 50–59 / 60–69 / 70+.
  — [Smart Marketer](https://smartmarketer.com/2025-facebook-insights-part-2/)
- Legibility for older readers: see Section 5.

### Inferences
- For a 55+ health or supplement audience, the evidence supports native-looking statics, with three provisos. The Frontiers sample was about 25 years old, and Applica flags trust-sensitive categories as places where polish can win. Health and supplements are trust-sensitive, so "native but credible" is the safer hypothesis to test: a real-person or real-kitchen photo, with clear, large type and a recognisable product, rather than deliberately crude. Use relatable older models.
- Health claims also carry Meta ad-policy constraints, for example on before/after imagery and implying personal attributes. That is outside this note's scope, but it limits some "native" formats such as testimonial screenshots.

### Gaps
- I found no published controlled A/B test (with a holdout or significance testing) of ugly vs polished statics.
- I found no study of ugly vs polished for 55+ audiences or for health/supplement products specifically.
- Barry Hott's primary posts and podcasts with numbers were not retrieved: the Northbeam webinar page returned 404 and X posts were not searched. Common Thread Collective, Pilothouse, Thesis and Jones Road case studies were not found in this pass.

## 5. Small-size legibility at real phone feed size

### Takeaway
A 1080-px-wide creative is shown about 375–430 pt wide, which is roughly a 2.5–2.9x downscale. Text must survive that reduction and be read in about 1–2 s. Research on glanceable reading favours larger, regular-width, non-lowercase type with high contrast. Older readers prefer larger type (14 pt beat 12 pt), and readability declines above a critical size. The "7 words" and "3 words per second" rules come from out-of-home advertising rules of thumb and are not validated research. I found no Meta-specific minimum type size.

### Cited Findings
- **MIT AgeLab (Sawyer et al. 2017), via NN/g**, glanceable word recognition:
  - Lowercase needed 26% more time for accurate reading than uppercase.
  - Condensed type needed 11.2% more time than regular width.
  - 4 mm text was read faster than 3 mm, and the lowercase penalty grows at small sizes.
  - All effects significant at p<0.01.
  NN/g recommends larger, non-condensed, non-lowercase text for glanceable content, with all-caps only for short isolated text. — [NN/g Glanceable Fonts](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/glanceable-fonts/)
- About 40% of phone sessions are microsessions of under 15 s (Ferreira et al. 2014). — [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/glanceable-fonts/)
- Glanceable reading is defined as reading in "extremely short periods of time, often less than one second." — [Readability Research review, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.09615)
- **Older readers:**
  - A 2022 systematic review found older adults prefer larger fonts, but "there exists a critical size at which readability declines." — [Frontiers in Psychology 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.931646/full)
  - A study of adults averaging 70 found 14-pt sans serif was rated more legible than 12-pt, with no typeface effect. — [ERAU](https://portfolio.erau.edu/en/publications/examining-perceptions-of-online-text-size-and-typeface-legibility/)
  - HHS guidance: 12–14 pt body and 18–20 pt headlines; avoid all-caps and busy backgrounds. — [ODPHP/HHS 2015](https://odphp.health.gov/news-archive/blog/2015/02/is-your-message-being-heard-part-three-getting/index.html)
- **Word count** (out-of-home rules of thumb, not validated):
  - 7 words or fewer.
  - "Drivers can process about three words per second" (unsourced).
  - Driver glances at billboards are often under 2 s (attributed to Virginia Tech Transportation Institute).

### Inferences
- **Scale math** (my calculation, not sourced): a 1080×1350 creative on a phone about 390 pt wide is scaled about 2.77x down. A 40-px-tall cap height becomes about 14 pt on screen, and 60 px becomes about 22 pt. For a 55+ audience, a reasonable floor is about 48–60 px cap height on a 1080-wide canvas for the headline, and about 36+ px for any supporting text.
- **"Squint test":** shrink the creative to about 30–35% (actual feed size) or blur it. The single focal element and the headline should still be identifiable. This is practitioner heuristic, not measured.
- **Word budget:** at about 0.25–2 s of attention and roughly 3–4 words per second of glance reading (unvalidated), a static can reliably deliver about 3–7 words in the stop window. Longer text needs the stop to happen first.

### Gaps
- I found no Meta-published minimum font size or legibility guidance for feed statics.
- I found no eye-tracking measurement of how many words people read in a static feed ad in 1–2 s.
- I found no study that quantifies the "squint test."
