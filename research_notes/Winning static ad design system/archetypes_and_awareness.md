# Static Ad Archetypes for DTC/Supplement Brands on Meta, and How Strategists Match Design to Awareness Level and Message Type

Scope: Meta static image ads, US market, 2022–2026. Research date: 2026-10-15. Most sources are practitioner newsletters, agency or vendor blogs, and creative-tool taxonomies (Motion, Atria-powered analyses). **I found no controlled, published per-format CTR/CPA benchmarks.** Nearly every performance claim is a practitioner's anecdote or a tool vendor's claim, and is labelled as such below. The Meta Ad Library lookup tool (TrendTrack) returned "not enough credits", so I could not pull or describe live ads from Balance of Nature, Relief Factor, Prevagen and similar brands. That gap is noted under the relevant question.

---

## Q1. The archetype catalog: what each looks like, and which awareness stage and message type it serves

### Takeaway
Practitioner taxonomies agree on a stable core of about 12–20 static archetypes. Three sources map these to funnel and awareness stages and largely agree with each other: the Motion team's 46-format library, Curtis Howland's 12-format system from 67,852 ads, and the coreyhaines31 tiered template library. In that mapping, organic-mimic formats (notes app, text or chat screenshots, post-it notes, memes, native text overlay, letters) serve TOF, meaning unaware to problem-aware. Comparison, callout, listicle, before/after and stat formats serve MOF, meaning solution-aware to product-aware. Review, offer and product-hero formats serve BOF, meaning most aware. Founder or letter formats are a notable exception: two sources rank them as cold-traffic scalers.

### Cited Findings

**Funnel definitions used by Motion's taxonomy.** TOF = Unaware to Problem-Aware; MOF = Solution-Aware to Product-Aware; BOF = Most Aware. — [Motion team visual-formats skill (via claudeskills.info, updated Jun 11, 2026)](https://claudeskills.info/zh/skills/motion-team/creative-strategy-skills/visual-formats/)

**Dataset context.** Curtis Howland classified 67,852 active ads from 106 brands (Jun 12, 2026). Statics were 55.6% of all ads and 64.8% among DTC brands. Image share by brand: Obvi 82.1%, Huel 67.8%, Glossier 42.8%, SHEIN 96.6%. The piece reports no per-format performance metrics, and it is sponsored by Notch, an AI ad tool. — [Curtis Howland, "The DTC static ad system"](https://newsletter.curtishowland.com/p/the-dtc-static-ad-system-what-67852)
- A separate analysis covered 40,000+ creatives from 11 nine- and ten-figure DTC brands, tracked with Atria (Apr 2025). Image share: Obvi 72.3%, RYZE 70.6%, Huel 63.1%, ŌURA 62.5%, Ridge 60.8%, AG1 45.1%, Dr. Squatch 23%. — [The Performers, Aazar Shad](https://www.blog.theperformers.io/p/9-figure-dtc-report)

**Archetype-by-archetype (visual description + stage + message type):**

- **Us-vs-Them / comparison table.** Two columns. The competitor or "old way" sits on the left, grayed out; your product sits on the right in full color. 4–6 rows with check and cross marks. Mid-funnel; "converts well for people already weighing you against an alternative, but rarely the format that opens cold net-new reach." The source says it "reliably lands in top 8" but gives no figures. — [coreyhaines31 static-ad-templates](https://github.com/coreyhaines31/marketingskills/blob/main/skills/ad-creative/references/static-ad-templates.md)
  - Howland tags it TOF/MOF: "side-by-side or chart comparison vs. competitor, old way, or category default." Example brand: Ridge. Test variants: generic "old way" vs. implied competitor vs. direct name. Best for products displacing an incumbent. — [Howland](https://newsletter.curtishowland.com/p/the-dtc-static-ad-system-what-67852)
  - Motion tags it MOF and notes that naming competitors can trigger Meta policy flags. — [Motion visual-formats](https://claudeskills.info/zh/skills/motion-team/creative-strategy-skills/visual-formats/)
  - Succession Media maps "comparison static, ingredient explainer" to solution-aware: "sells the difference in mechanism." Example: "Not all magnesium is the same. Here is why we use glycinate." — [Succession Media, Sep 2026](https://succession.media/guides/schwartz-awareness-levels-meta-ads/)
  - Alex Cooper (Adcrate) lists comparison ads (Us vs. Them, Before vs. After, "Me now vs. Future me") as one of the four categories most static winners fall into. His advice: rank the failed solutions customers have tried, using how often reviews mention each. — [Alex Cooper, "How to make better static ads in 2026," Jun 16, 2026](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/how-to-make-better-static-ads-in-2026)
  - Howland's earlier piece says comparison statics are "suited to supplements and skincare." — [Howland, Jan 30, 2026](https://newsletter.curtishowland.com/p/ive-spent-100m-on-meta-ads-in-the)
  - At the brand level, Ridge, Loop and Grüns use side-by-side product comparison visuals. — [The Performers](https://www.blog.theperformers.io/p/9-figure-dtc-report)

- **Checklist / listicle / "3 signs" / "reasons why."** Text-heavy list of 3–5 benefits, a "mini landing page." MOF. Best for supplements, meal replacements and subscriptions. Example: Huel. Test variants: 3 vs. 5 reasons. — [Howland](https://newsletter.curtishowland.com/p/the-dtc-static-ad-system-what-67852)
  - Numbered rows, each with an icon and a short line, product anchor at the bottom. The coreyhaines library rates this tier E ("decayed"; "avoid leading a batch with it") and calls the educational infographic "the healthier version." — [coreyhaines31](https://github.com/coreyhaines31/marketingskills/blob/main/skills/ad-creative/references/static-ad-templates.md)
  - Motion: Listicle is MOF. It co-occurs with Notes App about 50% of the time. — [Motion Notes App format page](https://motionapp.com/library/formats/notes-app)
  - Howland's "Text-Heavy" static, built as multiple text blocks in a landing-page style, is "for offer-driven campaigns." — [Howland Jan 2026](https://newsletter.curtishowland.com/p/ive-spent-100m-on-meta-ads-in-the)

- **Notes app / iPhone Notes.** "A screenshot of text styled to look like it was typed in the iPhone Notes app": yellow-accented Notes UI, typed system font, candid. Static only, TOF–MOF. Use for a confessional voice, founder POV, hot takes, or "things I wish I knew before." Brands seen running it: Calm, Noom, Our Place, Spacegoods, Vegamour, Happy Mammoth, Orgain, Blueland, SoFi, Reframe, Headway, Rise Science, Nutrition Geeks. Statistic and Listicle formats co-occur with it about 50% of the time. — [Motion format library: Notes App](https://motionapp.com/library/formats/notes-app)
  - A Telegram trends roundup lists "fake-native screenshot styled like a notes app or a text thread, not a banner" as a winning static pattern. No numbers given. — [CreativeRadar (Telegram)](https://t.me/s/CreativeRadar/25)

- **Tweet / Reddit / comment screenshot (comment response).** "Native-looking post card with avatar, handle, timestamp, engagement counts." Often used as the first frame. Mid-funnel; "strongest as a hook." The source says it is effective at roughly $100k–250k/month spend, without data. Must be real posts. — [coreyhaines31](https://github.com/coreyhaines31/marketingskills/blob/main/skills/ad-creative/references/static-ad-templates.md)
  - Motion's "Comment Response" (MOF–BOF) shows an on-screen social comment being answered. — [Motion visual-formats](https://claudeskills.info/zh/skills/motion-team/creative-strategy-skills/visual-formats/)
  - Huel's signature "reply to comment" UGC-native static answers objections such as "is it actually worth the money?" — [Howland](https://newsletter.curtishowland.com/p/the-dtc-static-ad-system-what-67852)
  - Howland's "UGC-Native / Screenshot" format (TOF) "looks organic: selfies, text screenshots, comment replies, phone photos." Test variants: selfie + text vs. text screenshot vs. comment reply. — [Howland](https://newsletter.curtishowland.com/p/the-dtc-static-ad-system-what-67852)

- **iMessage / text thread.** Motion's "Text Message" static is an SMS or chat-style mockup, TOF–MOF. A ChatGPT-conversation mockup is a separate TOF–MOF format. — [Motion visual-formats](https://claudeskills.info/zh/skills/motion-team/creative-strategy-skills/visual-formats/)
  - A 9:16 *video* version, where an iMessage thread unfolds bubble by bubble and ends on a brand and promo-code reveal, is documented secondhand. — [newreleases.io changelog, coreyhaines31 marketingskills](https://newreleases.io/project/github/coreyhaines31/marketingskills/release/v2.8.10)

- **Before/after.** Split frame (horizontal, vertical, or slider overlay) with an arrow or divider and minimal copy labelling each state "in customer language." MOF; Motion says MOF–BOF. Problem-aware buyers. Example: Obvi. Regulated or restricted by Meta in health, skincare and weight-loss. — [Howland](https://newsletter.curtishowland.com/p/the-dtc-static-ad-system-what-67852); [coreyhaines31](https://github.com/coreyhaines31/marketingskills/blob/main/skills/ad-creative/references/static-ad-templates.md); [Motion visual-formats](https://claudeskills.info/zh/skills/motion-team/creative-strategy-skills/visual-formats/)

- **Label callout / ingredient zoom / feature callout.** Product centered, with 3–5 (or 4–6) labels connected by leader lines or arrows, each 2–6 words. Mid-funnel. "Cheap and fast to iterate." Source callouts from attributes that recur in reviews. — [coreyhaines31](https://github.com/coreyhaines31/marketingskills/blob/main/skills/ad-creative/references/static-ad-templates.md)
  - Howland: "Product image with annotated benefit callouts and arrows," MOF. Supplements and functional products; Dr. Squatch, Huel. Test variants: 3 vs. 5 callouts vs. a full ingredient breakdown. — [Howland](https://newsletter.curtishowland.com/p/the-dtc-static-ad-system-what-67852)
  - Motion: Feature Benefit Callout is MOF–BOF. — [Motion visual-formats](https://claudeskills.info/zh/skills/motion-team/creative-strategy-skills/visual-formats/)
  - Dara Denney lists "feature-benefit callouts" among five ad formats that work, alongside "golden nugget" reviews, founder stories, statistics and educational ads. — [OMG Commerce Ecommerce Evolution podcast ep. 287](https://www.omgcommerce.com/ecommerce-evolution-podcast/crafting-ad-creative-that-converts-combining-data-boldness)

- **Offer stack / bundle fan / grid.** Bold typography, discount codes, urgency language, a clear CTA. BOF. Test variants: countdown vs. scarcity vs. exclusivity. Ridge's launch offers reportedly drive incremental ROAS above 4x. — [Howland](https://newsletter.curtishowland.com/p/the-dtc-static-ad-system-what-67852)
  - Grid static: 4–9 product tiles on neutral ground with consistent lighting, a small logo and an optional bundle price. Ranked tier A, a "cold-scaler," and the first static to test with a product range. Called a top performer at a 9-figure brand, with no figures. — [coreyhaines31](https://github.com/coreyhaines31/marketingskills/blob/main/skills/ad-creative/references/static-ad-templates.md)
  - Alex Cooper: offer-based ads (offers, collections, bundles) "capture existing demand" and are "less incremental than acquisition statics." — [Alex Cooper Jun 2026](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/how-to-make-better-static-ads-in-2026)
  - An anecdotal account of statics beating video lists the shared elements of the winners: a five-star rating bar at the top, urgency badges, and a slashed price with a clear CTA. — [Admetrics](https://www.admetrics.io/post/five-static-ad-formats-outperforming-video-in-2026) (single anecdote)

- **Advertorial / news headline ("native article" look) and press.** Motion "Press" (MOF–BOF): third-party media logos, headlines or quotes. Motion "Billboard" (TOF–MOF): styled as outdoor signage to signal scale. — [Motion visual-formats](https://claudeskills.info/zh/skills/motion-team/creative-strategy-skills/visual-formats/)
  - coreyhaines31 rates Press Mention tier F ("avoid") unless the coverage is licensed, because logo use carries rights risk. — [coreyhaines31](https://github.com/coreyhaines31/marketingskills/blob/main/skills/ad-creative/references/static-ad-templates.md)
  - Alex Cooper puts "publication or press mentions, post-it notes, native ads, comment responses" under organic-style statics, one of his four winning categories. — [Alex Cooper Jun 2026](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/how-to-make-better-static-ads-in-2026)
  - Succession Media maps "editorial static" plus an advertorial landing page to Unaware, and problem/explainer statics plus advertorial or listicle pages to Problem-Aware. — [Succession Media](https://succession.media/guides/schwartz-awareness-levels-meta-ads/)

- **Founder / doctor letter.** "Note-style layout, founder name/photo, no product glamour shot." Tier S cold-scaler, "the most reliable first top performer" and "first format to try for fresh reach." A sales-period variant also works. — [coreyhaines31](https://github.com/coreyhaines31/marketingskills/blob/main/skills/ad-creative/references/static-ad-templates.md)
  - Origin Story (tier S): portrait or team photo, 2–3 short paragraphs, product secondary. — same source.
  - Motion "Letter" (static, TOF–MOF): a long-form personal note addressed to the viewer. — [Motion visual-formats](https://claudeskills.info/zh/skills/motion-team/creative-strategy-skills/visual-formats/)
  - Dara Denney advises introducing the founder "when it fits the brand's storyline." — [Motion keynote, Oct 2025](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/why-most-creative-strategists-are-set-up-to-fail-and-how-to-fix-it/)
  - Succession Media maps founder video and review statics to Product-Aware. — [Succession Media](https://succession.media/guides/schwartz-awareness-levels-meta-ads/)

- **Handwritten sticky note / post-it / ugly / MS Paint.** "One scrappy element (sticky note, marker note, screenshot) over the product or plain ground." Described as strong during sales periods (BFCM, launches, flash sales) and "not an always-on default." — [coreyhaines31](https://github.com/coreyhaines31/marketingskills/blob/main/skills/ad-creative/references/static-ad-templates.md)
  - Howland's "Ugly / Lo-Fi / MS Paint" (TOF): handwritten text, crude drawings, mismatched fonts. Test variants: MS Paint vs. handwritten paper vs. phone notes. Example: Dirty Dog Farm. — [Howland](https://newsletter.curtishowland.com/p/the-dtc-static-ad-system-what-67852)
  - Motion "Post It" is TOF–MOF. — [Motion visual-formats](https://claudeskills.info/zh/skills/motion-team/creative-strategy-skills/visual-formats/)

- **Big bold headline + product (product image static / headline statement / product hero).** Alex Cooper: "a product shot with a headline and benefits, or a headline alone... has produced more winners for his team than any other," often using a customer review as the headline. — [Alex Cooper Jun 2026](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/how-to-make-better-static-ads-in-2026)
  - coreyhaines31 "Headline Statement": one dominant text line taking more than 60% of the frame, a single product hero, minimal background, small logo. Mid-funnel. — [coreyhaines31](https://github.com/coreyhaines31/marketingskills/blob/main/skills/ad-creative/references/static-ad-templates.md)
  - Howland "Product Hero" (BOF, retargeting): a clean product photo on a neutral or brand background. Test variants: brand color vs. lifestyle vs. pure white. — [Howland](https://newsletter.curtishowland.com/p/the-dtc-static-ad-system-what-67852)
  - RYZE runs "simple statics with bold benefits"; Obvi runs strong text overlays and color pops. — [The Performers](https://www.blog.theperformers.io/p/9-figure-dtc-report)

- **Infographic / mechanism diagram.** A diagram, cycle or labelled cross-section, with minimal branding until the end. Mid-funnel; "reads as content rather than an ad." — [coreyhaines31](https://github.com/coreyhaines31/marketingskills/blob/main/skills/ad-creative/references/static-ad-templates.md)
  - An annotated diagram is one of five native image directions. For problem-aware, solution-unknown viewers: "imply a mechanism with a 'here's what's happening' visual." — [adlibrary.com native image ads guide, Aug 2026](https://adlibrary.com/guides/native-image-ads-guide)

- **Testimonial quote card with photo.** Social Proof/Testimonial (MOF/BOF): customer quotes, star ratings, review counts or expert endorsements with product imagery. Supplements, skincare. Test variants: customer review vs. expert vs. press. — [Howland](https://newsletter.curtishowland.com/p/the-dtc-static-ad-system-what-67852)
  - Testimonial Stack: three vertical rows, each with photo, name and a one-line quote. Tier E, used only if each quote addresses a different objection. — [coreyhaines31](https://github.com/coreyhaines31/marketingskills/blob/main/skills/ad-creative/references/static-ad-templates.md)
  - Howland's 2026 guidance: Social Proof "when trust is the barrier." — [Howland Jan 2026](https://newsletter.curtishowland.com/p/ive-spent-100m-on-meta-ads-in-the)

- **Stat callout.** "One giant number filling ~60% of the frame, one line of context, product/logo anchor." Tier C, situational. — [coreyhaines31](https://github.com/coreyhaines31/marketingskills/blob/main/skills/ad-creative/references/static-ad-templates.md)
  - Howland Data/Stat Callout (MOF): "one number as the image's hero," for supplements and clinical products. Example: Obvi. Test variants: social-proof number vs. spec vs. outcome number. — [Howland](https://newsletter.curtishowland.com/p/the-dtc-static-ad-system-what-67852)
  - Motion Statistic is MOF–BOF. — [Motion](https://claudeskills.info/zh/skills/motion-team/creative-strategy-skills/visual-formats/)
  - A third-party tier list ranks statistics ads C-tier, "suitable for brand awareness but less effective for direct ROI." This is a secondary summary of a YouTube tier list, and the author is unclear. — [videohighlight.com summary](https://videohighlight.com/v/KmbEjy0UBT8)

- **Meme.** Trending templates, pop culture, humor. TOF. Example: Dr. Squatch. — [Howland](https://newsletter.curtishowland.com/p/the-dtc-static-ad-system-what-67852). - **POV / native text overlay / selfie.** Motion "Native Text Overlay" (static, TOF–MOF): a casual photo with native-style text as the hook. "Selfie" (TOF–MOF). "Unconventional Text Placement" (TOF): text on an unexpected surface. "Sign" (TOF–BOF): a person holding a sign with a USP or CTA. — [Motion visual-formats](https://claudeskills.info/zh/skills/motion-team/creative-strategy-skills/visual-formats/)

- **Review screenshot with stars.** Mimics the review UI where buyers actually read reviews (Amazon, Trustpilot, App Store), with five stars, reviewer name and date. Tier E; "reserve for a single standout review." — [coreyhaines31](https://github.com/coreyhaines31/marketingskills/blob/main/skills/ad-creative/references/static-ad-templates.md)
  - Motion "Review" (static) is BOF. — [Motion](https://claudeskills.info/zh/skills/motion-team/creative-strategy-skills/visual-formats/)
  - Succession Media: review static → Product-Aware, "removes the last objection." — [Succession Media](https://succession.media/guides/schwartz-awareness-levels-meta-ads/)

- **Problem image (no product) / problem-solution.** Two zones, with the pain at the top and the product below as the answer.   - For unaware viewers: "Show a symptom the reader will recognize. The ad names something before it sells anything." The "caught in the moment" candid photo is one of the native directions. — [adlibrary.com](https://adlibrary.com/guides/native-image-ads-guide)
  - Succession Media: unaware creative "looks like an organic post or editorial piece," and the product "appears late, after the problem is introduced." — [Succession Media](https://succession.media/guides/schwartz-awareness-levels-meta-ads/)

**Where sources disagree:**
- **Founder/letter.** coreyhaines31 ranks it the #1 cold scaler. Motion puts Letter at TOF–MOF and Founder video at TOF–MOF. Succession Media puts founder content at Product-Aware.
- **Us-vs-Them.** Howland says TOF/MOF; coreyhaines31 and Motion say MOF only.
- **Reviews and testimonial stacks.** coreyhaines31 calls them "decayed." Howland and Curtis Howland's 2026 piece still list Social Proof as a core conversion format.

### Inferences
- Combining the three taxonomies gives a usable default mapping:
  - **Unaware:** native text overlay or candid problem photo, notes app, meme, letter or advertorial-look static, ugly/post-it.
  - **Problem-aware:** problem/solution split, "3 signs" listicle, mechanism infographic, tweet/Reddit/comment screenshot.
  - **Solution-aware:** us-vs-them, ingredient callout, "not all X are the same" mechanism comparison, stat callout.
  - **Product-aware:** review screenshot, testimonial card, before/after (where policy allows), FAQ card, press.
  - **Most aware:** offer stack or bundle fan, product hero, grid with price.
- **Message-type mapping:**
  - native/advertorial → letter, notes app, editorial static
  - problem-agitation → problem/solution, listicle "signs"
  - mechanism → infographic, callout
  - proof → stat, review, press, before/after
  - offer → offer stack, bundle
  - comparison → us-vs-them
  - testimonial → quote card, comment response
- The Motion data on Notes App co-occurring with Statistic and Listicle about 50% of the time suggests that hybrid formats are common in practice, e.g. a notes-app "3 signs" list with a stat. This matches Alex Cooper's remark that "winners often combine these formats."

### Gaps
- No source published per-archetype CTR, CPA or hook-rate benchmarks. Tier rankings (coreyhaines31) and "most winners" claims (Alex Cooper) are qualitative practitioner judgments.
- The "receipt / cost breakdown" archetype was not described in any source I found. It is likely a variant of the stat or comparison format (e.g., "$4/day vs. $X at the coffee shop"), but that is unsourced.
- "POV" as a static, as distinct from video, was not described. Motion's closest formats are Native Text Overlay and Selfie.
- I could not fetch live Meta Ad Library examples for the named brands (AG1, Arrae, Bloom, Momentous, Ancient Nutrition, Balance of Nature, Prevagen, Relief Factor) because TrendTrack credits ran out. No brand-specific static descriptions were verified for these.

## Q2. How practitioners choose visual treatment by awareness level and angle

### Takeaway
No named practitioner publishes a single "awareness → visual" rulebook. The consistent practitioner logic has five parts:
1. Lead with relevance to someone who doesn't know or care about the brand (Barry Hott, Alex Cooper).
2. Match the look of organic feed content rather than competitors' ads (Hott).
3. Increase product prominence and proof as awareness rises (Succession Media, Motion's funnel labels).
4. Treat awareness level as a tagged creative variable across many diverse concepts rather than a campaign split (2026 commentary).
5. Prefer full-funnel ads that open cold and close warm (Hott).

### Cited Findings
- **Barry Hott, as relayed by Alex Cooper (Apr 10, 2025).**
  - "Nobody cares what you have to say unless it relates to them clearly and instantly."
  - Make "ugly" ads that match what the audience watches organically: "Pretty ads stand out and get skipped" (Hott, 2022 post).
  - Study organic content, not competitors, to avoid a "sea of sameness," and use burner accounts to see what the audience is served.
  - "Clear beats clever."
  - Hook relevance matters more than hook rate; "some winning ads had hook rates under 15%."
  - Make "full funnel ads" rather than separate cold and warm ads.
  - Hott claims about $1B in spend since 2008; another profile says $600M+.
  - — [Alex Cooper, "7 Facebook advertising lessons I learned from Barry Hott"](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/7-facebook-advertising-lessons-i-learned-from-barry-hott); [Practical Ecommerce](https://www.practicalecommerce.com/?p=1539710); [Northbeam webinar](https://northbeam.io/post/unleashing-the-power-of-ugly-ads) (Northbeam claims ugly ads "consistently perform better" but shows no data)
- **Alex Cooper (Adcrate, not Pilothouse/Rebel Ads; Adcrate works with Huel, Licorice.com and The Perfect Jean). Jun 16, 2026.**
  - **TOF headline test:** is the headline "immediately relevant to someone who doesn't know or care about the brand?" Example: "My confidence is back" is weaker than "The only shampoo that ended my dandruff battle."
  - **Simple bold USP callouts** rather than clever or technical claims.
  - **"Psychology-based design":** give elements intentional size and placement, because statics are scanned in a fraction of a second. Use text warps or distortions ("adds a half-second of attention"), highlights, arrows, bold and underline.
  - **Reading order:** headline → qualifier → product → social proof → primary text. Example: a Meno ad with a large headline, a qualifying subline, the product, and "3,600 reviews."
  - **Customer language:** mine reviews and post-purchase surveys for headlines; emotive review headlines "have spent six figures, some running for years."
  - **Persona discovery:** cross-reference reviews, ad comments and surveys against the account. In one account an underserved persona now makes up 3 of the top 5 ads.
  - **Competitor research:** sort Meta Ad Library results by impressions rather than longest-running.
  - **Strong static brands named:** O-Positive, Obvi, Huel, Oats Overnight.
  - — [Alex Cooper Jun 2026](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/how-to-make-better-static-ads-in-2026); [Alex Cooper on ChatGPT-4o statics (Mar 2025)](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/how-to-make-static-ads-with-chatgpt-4o-plus-some-freebies)
- **Succession Media (Yassine Chakir, Sep 2026): explicit Schwartz → Meta creative mapping for supplements.**
  - **Unaware:** UGC story, native video or editorial static. Product appears late. → advertorial.
  - **Problem-aware:** problem/explainer static that "names the problem in general terms and explains why it happens." → advertorial or listicle.
  - **Solution-aware:** comparison static or ingredient explainer that "sells the difference in mechanism." Product is central.
  - **Product-aware:** review static or founder content that "removes the last objection (proof, guarantee)." Product is prominent with proof around it.
  - **Most aware:** offer static or catalog that "states the offer and a reason to act now."
  - **Compliance:** problem-aware hooks carry the most risk. Use "Why so many people wake up at 3am" rather than "Do you wake up at 3am every night?"
  - — [Succession Media](https://succession.media/guides/schwartz-awareness-levels-meta-ads/)
- **adlibrary.com (Aug 2026): native image directions by stage.**
  - Unaware → show a recognizable symptom.
  - Problem-aware → imply a mechanism.
  - Solution-aware → differentiation "shown rather than claimed."
  - "None is inherently better; the right choice depends on the angle and the awareness stage."
  - — [adlibrary.com](https://adlibrary.com/guides/native-image-ads-guide)
- **Awareness as a creative variable, and cold ≠ unaware.** One 2026 guide argues awareness level should be tagged on every ad and reported, not run as separate campaigns. Another recommends supplying creative across all five levels and letting delivery match people. Another notes that cold traffic is not automatically unaware in crowded categories. — [The Social Outline, "Awareness levels in Meta ads"](https://thesocialoutline.com/blog/awareness-levels-meta-ads); [Selzee](https://selzee.com/eugene-schwartz-5-levels-of-awareness) (search-summary level; not fetched in full)
- **Sarah Levinger** (secondhand Foreplay webinar summary, about Jan 2024; mark as older).
  - About 95% of decisions are made subconsciously, so visuals, color and language should match the audience's subconscious associations.
  - Imagery must align with the message.
  - Simplify complex ideas.
  - Levers: loss aversion ("If you can make your claim clear and like risk-free, that is like the sweet spot"), herd mentality (testimonials, UGC), positive framing ("Enjoy a better night's sleep" over "Don't suffer from poor sleep"), anchoring (strikethrough price), and priming through consistent imagery.
  - Customer-language anecdote: switching from "nootropic coffee" to "mushroom coffee" fixed fatigue.
  - Her later work frames ads as an identity "mirror."
  - — [Foreplay blog on Sarah Levinger](https://www.foreplay.co/post/harnessing-psychology-in-creative-strategy-sarah-levingers-insights-on-consumer-behavior); identity-mirror framing is from a profile surfaced in search ([Foreplay expert page](https://www.foreplay.co/experts/sarah-levinger)); not fetched in full
- **Dara Denney (Motion Chief Evangelist).**
  - Five formats that work: feature-benefit callouts, "golden nugget" reviews, founder stories, statistics, educational.
  - She breaks down press screenshots vs. statistics vs. founder's story "and which works best when." The event page has no specifics.
  - Warning: "assuming messaging that wins in UGC will automatically win in static formats is a mistake."
  - Case: a Laura Geller ad with $2M+ spend and $6M+ revenue (format not specified in the summary).
  - — [OMG Commerce podcast](https://www.omgcommerce.com/ecommerce-evolution-podcast/crafting-ad-creative-that-converts-combining-data-boldness); [Motion event](https://motionapp.com/event/maximize-creative-performance-round-up-of-top-trends-and-formats); [Motion talk, Feb 2025](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/this-one-ad-format-wins-for-dara-denney-s-clients/)
- **Message–image alignment and "one idea."** A static "either communicates one idea instantly or it loses the impression." The headline carries the benefit and the image earns the stop. — [Hawky.ai](https://hawky.ai/blog/static-ads-that-convert) (vendor blog)
- **Brand pattern evidence (Atria-tracked).**
  - Many winning ads are "unbranded, Native, and Minimal."
  - Strong headline plus clear subtext.
  - Brand hooks: "If your tongue is white…" (RYZE); "Why I stopped replacing cookware every year" (HexClad).

  - — [The Performers](https://www.blog.theperformers.io/p/9-figure-dtc-report)

### Inferences
- The practical decision rule shared by these sources: the colder the viewer, the more the static should look like organic content (screenshots, notes, candid photos, editorial). In that case the image names the viewer's problem or identity, and the product is small or absent. The warmer the viewer, the more the static should look like a designed ad: product large, proof elements (stars, review count, press) and price or offer visible.
- Product prominence and branding scale up with awareness level. This is the most consistent visual variable across Succession Media, adlibrary.com, Motion's funnel tags and Hott.

## Q3. Advertorial / native statics for older (50+) audiences

### Takeaway
Sources agree on what makes a static look "native":
- candid rather than studio photography
- screenshot or UI mocks
- text over a photo
- no logo lockup or CTA button in the image
- copy that reads as a story or observation
- an editorial-style landing page after the click

**I found no published data showing advertorial statics outperform for 50+ supplement buyers.** The age-specific evidence is thin and comes from vendor sources.

### Cited Findings
- **Native cues:**
  - "candid framing instead of studio photography"
  - "screenshot-style UI mocks"
  - "text-over-photo layouts"
  - "no obvious logo lockup or CTA button treatment in the image"
  - "copy that reads as a story or observation rather than a pitch"
  - Five directions: screenshot UI mock, "caught in the moment" candid photo, before/after split, annotated diagram, text-over-photo editorial layout. The page does **not** mention serif fonts or news-site styling, and it has no performance numbers or age claims.
  - — [adlibrary.com native image ads guide (Aug 2026)](https://adlibrary.com/guides/native-image-ads-guide)
- **Advertorial definition and destination.** "A story-style ad designed to look and read like editorial content rather than a promotion." A native image ad has "no banner and no hard sell in frame one." The landing page is "styled as editorial content, an article rather than a sales page." The native look does not exempt the ad from Meta ad standards and FTC native-advertising rules. — [adlibrary.com](https://adlibrary.com/guides/native-image-ads-guide); [Senior Living Smart](https://seniorlivingsmart.com/blog/senior-living-marketing-strategy-what-is-an-advertorial/) ("formatted like a talk show or news feature"; "facts over spin")
- **Supplement awareness mapping.** Unaware and problem-aware supplement ads send to an advertorial or listicle (the example advertorial is about 2,000 words). — [Succession Media](https://succession.media/guides/schwartz-awareness-levels-meta-ads/)
- **News-style native (Taboola guidance, adapting social ads to publisher placements).** Headlines of 40–50 characters (60 max); "publisher language" rather than sales language; a single subject such as a face or an item with no text. Example: OTTY Sleep's emoji-heavy 80-character Facebook headline rewritten as an editorial line. — [Realize/Taboola guide](https://realize.com/marketing-hub/repurposing-your-facebook-ads-for-taboola-a-practical-guide/); [The Drum / Taboola](https://thedrum.com/profile/taboola/news/new-to-native-advertising-5-ways-to-adapt-social-media-ads-for-native). This is open-web native guidance, not Meta.
- **Older-audience evidence (weak, vendor sources):**
  - A practitioner blog reports that 40+ audiences "work even with no headline copy." — [Magic Library native ad examples](https://www.magiclibrary.co/blog/native-ad-examples) (search snippet; not verified)
  - A senior-care native example pairs a news-style trend title with a warm photo of a senior and caregiver. — same source
  - "8% of baby boomers trust social media ads" is attributed to Oracle, secondhand and repeated across blogs. Treat as directional. — [Disruptive Digital](https://disruptivedigital.agency/reaching-older-demographics/)
  - LoopMe survey: 37% of 45–54s and 40% of 55–64s buy from online ads at least every few months. — [Business Wire / LoopMe, Nov 2024](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241112504126/en)
  - People in their 70s and 80s respond to images that mirror who they are; "negativity does not engage this audience." This is a senior-living agency claim. — [Cardinal Digital / senior living agencies](https://www.cardinaldigitalmarketing.com/healthcare-specialties/senior-living-agency/paid-social-facebook-advertising/)
  - Plain language over hype, and avoid age-related wording or slang. — [Quimby Digital](https://quimbydigital.com/marketing-to-baby-boomers/); [AdShark](https://adshark.com/blog/marketing-to-baby-boomers/)

### Inferences
- **Native = "does not look like an ad."** For a Facebook feed, that means matching the organic posts a 50+ user actually sees: shared news-article link previews, photo-plus-caption posts, screenshots, and text-on-photo images. Serif news headlines and photojournalism imagery follow from that logic. However, **no source I found explicitly documented serif fonts or "news-site" styling as a tested driver**, so this is inference.
- Given the reported low trust in social ads among boomers, the cues likely to transfer are positive, plain-language, editorial and proof-forward ones. Aggressive problem-agitation may disengage this audience and is also the highest-compliance-risk zone for supplements (Succession Media). This is unverified as a performance claim.

### Gaps
- No case studies or numbers for advertorial-style Meta statics from Balance of Nature, Prevagen, Relief Factor, Blissy, Smart for Life or similar 50+ DR supplement brands. The Ad Library lookup failed for lack of credits, and web searches surfaced none.
- No source tested serif vs. sans-serif headlines, or "Facebook post" mockups vs. designed ads, for 50+ audiences.

## Q4. Design differences for long vs. short primary copy, and cold vs. retargeting

### Takeaway
Practitioners say the image must work alone, because most viewers never expand the caption. Long primary copy (problem-agitate-solve, letter-style) pairs with colder, story-led statics. Short copy plus offer-forward statics belong in retargeting. Hott and Cooper push "full-funnel" statics that work for both.

### Cited Findings
- **Short primary text for retargeting and hot audiences.** Statics "frequently outperform video for retargeting and brand-aware audiences." For colder viewers, a longer PAS caption can support a static, "but the image has to work alone because most viewers never expand the caption." — [Hawky.ai](https://hawky.ai/blog/static-ads-that-convert) (vendor)
- **Test both copy lengths.** Short-form is about 150 characters, which displays without "See more." — [Meta copywriting transcript (GoTranscript)](https://gotranscript.com/public/meta-copywriting-tips-short-vs-long-form-best-practices-and-testing)
- **Reels/Stories need concise text; Feed allows more.** — [Adamigo copy-length guide](https://www.adamigo.ai/blog/ultimate-meta-ad-copy-length-testing-guide)
- **Curtis Howland (Jan 2026):**
  - TOF statics should be "benefit-driven, curiosity hooks, lifestyle imagery," aimed at audience expansion. "Avoid heavy promotions; save discounts for retargeting."
  - "Text-Heavy" mini-landing-page statics suit offer-driven campaigns.
  - "Specificity beats vague" ("lost 23 lbs").
  - "Ugly ads often beat polished"; product in use beats product on a table.
  - — [Howland Jan 2026](https://newsletter.curtishowland.com/p/ive-spent-100m-on-meta-ads-in-the)
- **Full-funnel ads (Barry Hott).** Open with content relevant to cold viewers, then move them to action. Example: an Opopop ad that worked for both audiences. — [Alex Cooper on Barry Hott](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/7-facebook-advertising-lessons-i-learned-from-barry-hott)
- **Offer statics capture existing demand and are less incremental.** — [Alex Cooper Jun 2026](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/how-to-make-better-static-ads-in-2026)
- **Reading order.** Cooper places primary text last in the reading order: headline → qualifier → product → social proof → primary text. This implies that the image carries the hook and the qualification. — same source
- **Batch guidance.**
  - Cold reach: weight toward founder message, origin story and grid static.
  - Warm/retargeting: callout, FAQ card, before/after, competitor callout.
  - After data accumulates: about 60% proven templates and 40% broader coverage.
  - — [coreyhaines31](https://github.com/coreyhaines31/marketingskills/blob/main/skills/ad-creative/references/static-ad-templates.md)
- **Meta specs.**
  - 1:1 at 1080×1080 and 4:5 at 1080×1350; keep content in safe zones; 9:16 for Stories/Reels. — [WarRoom knowledge base](https://services.warroominc.com/en/knowledgebase/meta-static-image-ads); [coreyhaines31 search summary](https://github.com/coreyhaines31/marketingskills/blob/main/skills/ad-creative/references/static-ad-templates.md)
  - The same WarRoom page warns that text over 20% "may experience reduced ad delivery." This conflicts with Meta's retirement of the 20% text rule around 2020–2021, which I did not verify here. Treat the WarRoom claim as possibly outdated.
  - Headlines of 5–7 words max and minimal text on the image are recommended. — [Verde Media 2026](https://verdemedia.com/blog/the-guide-to-meta-ads-creative-2026)
  - "4:5 feed format: +15% screen vs. 1:1" (no source given). — [Howland Jan 2026](https://newsletter.curtishowland.com/p/ive-spent-100m-on-meta-ads-in-the)

### Inferences
- **When primary copy is long** (letter or advertorial-style), the image should carry one hook line and look native: a notes app, a letter, an editorial photo. That makes clicking "See more" feel like reading a post rather than an ad.
- **When primary copy is short,** the image has to carry the entire argument. Use a headline statement, us-vs-them, callouts or a stat, because the image is the only argument.
- **For retargeting,** add proof and offer elements (stars, review count, price anchor, guarantee) directly into the image.

### Gaps
- No controlled data comparing long vs. short primary copy specifically for statics. Most guidance is vendor-blog consensus.
- No source quantified cold vs. retargeting performance by archetype.

## Q5. Iteration frameworks: "lock image, change headline," "1 concept → many hooks," and testing at volume

### Takeaway
The classic isolation method is still described: same image with a different headline, or the reverse. However, 2025–2026 practitioners increasingly argue that after Meta's Andromeda retrieval changes, near-duplicate statics (headline-only or color swaps) are treated as one creative. Their recommendation is to produce many *conceptually different* statics: new format, angle or persona. Small-variable tests are reserved for refining proven concepts.

### Cited Findings
- **Isolation pattern.** "Same image, different headline. Same headline, different image." If the image and headline both change, a win is "uninterpretable." Headline swaps belong to *refinement* testing on proven concepts. Copy and headline are "typically a smaller effect than the visual, but cheap to test." Testing five headlines that say the same thing yields minimal learning. — [First Pier](https://firstpier.com/resources/facebook-ads-creative-testing); [Segwise testing framework](https://segwise.ai/blog/facebook-ad-creative-testing-framework) (search summaries)
- **"End of lazy iteration" (Beyond ROAS, Nov 21, 2025).**
  - Headline swaps, background color changes and slight re-edits "no longer count as new creative."
  - "Meta's AI reads your ads visually, not just textually," so lookalikes fatigue together and pool data.
  - Recommendations: rotate themes (education, humor, proof, nostalgia), vary setting, framing, tone and message, use multiple creative teams, and track CPM as a creative-health metric.
  - The author is unnamed and gives no data. The claim about visual-similarity grouping is not confirmed by Meta documentation in my searches.
  - — [Beyond ROAS](https://beyondroas.beehiiv.com/p/the-end-of-lazy-creative-iteration)
- **Curtis Howland (Jan 2026).**
  - Run 8–15 conceptually different ads, not headline swaps.
  - Test the same hook with 5 different visuals.
  - "3-3-3 framework" (3 dimensions × 3 = 27 combinations).
  - Give new creative 5–7 days.
  - Kill at 2–3× target CPA with no purchase; wait for 10–12 purchases per concept.
  - Fatigue signals: CTR below 1%, a 20%+ week-over-week CTR decline, prospecting frequency above 3.0.
  - Refresh every 7–14 days at scale.
  - Production rate: 1 new ad per $3,000 of monthly spend.
  - Statics drive "60–70% of conversions for many brands" he works with; a typical mix is 60–70% statics.
  - Motion-attributed claim: "lo-fi content: 84% win rate" (unverified).
  - — [Howland Jan 2026](https://newsletter.curtishowland.com/p/ive-spent-100m-on-meta-ads-in-the)
- **Curtis Howland (Jun 2026), diversity framework.**
  - Top performers run 6–8 of the 12 formats; brands default to 2–3.
  - Five diversity axes: format, visual style (polished/lo-fi/editorial), angle, offer type, persona.
  - Healthy benchmark: 12–15 new statics per week. Teams produce 15–25 static variations per week vs. 3–5 videos. A static takes 30–60 minutes vs. 4–8 hours for a video.
  - Repurpose winners across formats: a video hook becomes a static headline, a testimonial becomes a Data Callout.
  - 10-question audit, e.g., "at least one ugly or meme ad in rotation," "oldest static refreshed in last 4 weeks."
  - — [Howland Jun 2026](https://newsletter.curtishowland.com/p/the-dtc-static-ad-system-what-67852)
- **Statics as fast message validators.** They "validate a message angle within as little as 72 hours of live spend." — [Admetrics](https://www.admetrics.io/post/five-static-ad-formats-outperforming-video-in-2026) (vendor)
- **Motion: finding winners "is a volume game."** — [Motion 2025 Creative Trends](https://motionapp.com/creative-trends/)
- **Dara Denney's KPI split.**
  - Primary KPIs: spend, results, cost per result, ROAS.
  - Storytelling KPIs: frequency, CPM, CTR, hook rate, hold rate.
  - When frequency reaches 3–4 within a few weeks, the creative may be tapping out (LinkedIn summary).
  - — [LinkedIn summary of Denney framework](https://ca.linkedin.com/in/em-b) (secondhand)
- **Testing thresholds (vendor rules of thumb).** About 50 optimization events, and 3–7 days, before reading results. 1,000+ impressions per variant for hook rate; 50–100 conversions per variant for CPA. Meta advises against using dynamic creative as a substitute for split tests. — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/facebook-ad-creative-testing-framework); [First Pier](https://firstpier.com/resources/facebook-ads-creative-testing)
- **Alex Cooper.** Do the basics well, then prioritize message testing. Use AI for volume to supplement strategy, not replace it. Generate the image without text and add the headline in a design tool (Mar 2025, tied to the model capabilities of the time). — [Alex Cooper Jun 2026](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/how-to-make-better-static-ads-in-2026); [Alex Cooper Mar 2025](https://alexcooper.beehiiv.com/p/how-to-make-static-ads-with-chatgpt-4o-plus-some-freebies)
- **Format variant tests to run within a concept** (Howland Jun 2026):
  - Before/after: horizontal vs. vertical vs. slider
  - Callout: 3 vs. 5 callouts
  - Stat: social proof vs. spec vs. outcome number
  - Ugly: MS Paint vs. handwritten vs. phone notes
  - Comparison: generic vs. implied vs. named competitor
  - Product hero: brand color vs. lifestyle vs. white background
  - — [Howland Jun 2026](https://newsletter.curtishowland.com/p/the-dtc-static-ad-system-what-67852)

### Inferences
- The current consensus resolves "lock image, change headline" vs. "1 concept → many hooks" into a two-tier system:
  1. **Exploration.** Many distinct concepts across formats, angles and personas, aiming for visual dissimilarity so Meta treats them as different creatives.
  2. **Refinement.** Once a concept wins, iterate headlines and hooks on it, ideally by re-skinning the winning message into new archetypes rather than making pixel-similar clones.

  This combines Howland, Beyond ROAS and First Pier/Segwise.

### Gaps
- No primary Meta documentation confirmed that Andromeda groups visually similar statics. This is a widely repeated practitioner claim.
- The "84% lo-fi win rate" and "statics = 60–70% of conversions" figures trace to Motion and Social Media Examiner via secondary posts. I did not verify the original sources.
- No published data from Foreplay, Atria or Marpipe on headline-swap vs. new-concept win rates was found.
